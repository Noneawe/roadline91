ROADLINE '91

A point-to-point racing game in the style of the early polygon racers of around 1990 – flat-shaded polygons, a 320×200 picture where every pixel is an index into a 256-color palette, Bayer dithering and FM music. Pure JavaScript and WebGL2, no libraries, no external assets.

Features

  • About 40 km route from the coast over a snowy pass, through a canyon, redwoods, suburbs, a lake and the highway to the desert – six sections ending at gas stations, two branches including a gravel shortcut.
  • Four fictional cars: Vektor GT (all-rounder), Aurora MR (fastest, nervous), Breeze R (best in corners), Kestrel S (stable, good in the rain).
  • Arcade-sim physics: torque curves, five gears with automatic or manual shifting, a bicycle tyre model with slides you can catch.
  • Two-way traffic, an AI rival that stops at the stations too, police with radars, chases and citations – three stops end the race.
  • Day, sunset and night; rain and fog; outside cameras; a replay of the last 60 seconds.
  • Original FM-synthesis music and sound effects generated with the Web Audio API: rpm-driven engine, siren with Doppler shift.
  • Records, key remapping, volume settings, touch controls, 640×400 enhanced mode and a 15 FPS historical mode (F3).

How it is made

The world is rendered into an R8UI 320×200 texture that holds palette indices, not colors. Flat shading, fog and lighting are all quantized with a 4×4 Bayer matrix in index space; a final pass looks the indices up in a 256-entry palette texture and scales the picture to a 4:3 letterbox with nearest-neighbour sampling. Models, sounds, music and the font are defined in code – there are no image or audio files. The whole game builds into a single HTML file of about 420 KB.
